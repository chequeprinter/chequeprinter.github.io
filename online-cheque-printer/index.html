What people mean by an online cheque printer

When someone in India searches for an online cheque printer, they usually want a practical way to fill a bank cheque on a computer and print it on a genuine cheque leaf. They already have leaves issued by SBI, HDFC, ICICI, Axis or another bank, and they want the date, payee, amount in words and amount in figures to land in the right places, neatly, without overwriting.

That is exactly what Cheque Printer does. You obtain it online from this site, install it on a Windows 10 or 11 PC, and print from your own printer. The “online” part is how you get the software. The printing itself happens locally, which is the safer design for a document that a bank will pay.

Why this is not a cheque printer in the browser

It can be tempting to put a cheque form on a web page and hit Print. We chose not to publish a hosted printer, for two reasons. First, a cheque carries sensitive business information: who you are paying, how much, and sometimes enough context to guess at cash flow. That data should stay on your computer, not in a browser tab or on a third-party server. Second, cheque layout and alignment are the product’s core logic. This public site contains no printing or alignment code.

The official download is the Windows desktop app. Use that to print. Use this site to learn how cheques work, to convert an amount into words, and to read bank-by-bank guides.

What the Cheque Printer app prints

Cheque Printer fills pre-printed CTS-2010 cheque leaves, the standard used in India’s Cheque Truncation System. On a typical leaf it prints:

  • The date, one digit in each of the eight DDMMYYYY boxes at the top right.
  • The payee name on the “Pay” line.
  • The amount in words in the Indian style, using lakh and crore, ending with “Only”, split across the two “Rupees” lines. You can preview the wording with the amount in words converter.
  • The amount in figures in the rupee box, with Indian digit grouping (for example 2,50,000.00).
  • An optional A/C Payee crossing in the top-left corner, so the cheque can be credited only to the named payee’s account.

It does not print your signature, and it does not print in the MICR band at the bottom of the leaf. You sign by hand after the ink is dry. The MICR line is reserved for the cheque number and bank codes that clearing systems read, and it must stay clear. For the official rules behind this layout, see the CTS-2010 explainer.

How an online cheque printer is used at home or in the office

At home, people typically print a handful of cheques a month: rent, school fees, a vendor, or a family payment that still has to go by cheque. You need a genuine leaf from your bank (never a photocopy), a Windows PC, and an inkjet or laser printer that can take a cheque through a rear or manual tray. The first time, you print a test on plain A4 paper, hold it over a real leaf against the light, and adjust if a field sits slightly off. After that, daily use is just type, preview and print. A walk-through for first-time use is in how to print a cheque at home or in the office. Alignment detail is in the printing and calibration guide.

In an office, a shop, a CA firm, a society or a school, the same app is used for a stack of cheques. Because everything runs offline, you can print even if the internet is down, and payee lists never go to a cloud account. Several people can use the portable build from a folder if your IT policy prefers that to an installer. There is no subscription and no per-cheque fee.

Print settings that actually matter

Most “the print is shifted or shrunk” problems are not about the software. They come from the Windows print dialog. Choose paper size A4, scale 100% or “Actual size” (never “Fit to page”), margins none, and turn headers and footers off. Feed the leaf the same way every time, using the rear or manual tray if you have one, either landscape (horizontal) or end-first (vertical). Use permanent black ink or toner so the writing does not smudge in the rain or in a bag.

Online cheque printer versus writing by hand

Handwriting is perfectly valid. Software is useful when you want consistent lettering, a correct amount in words every time, and no overwritten figures — important because CTS-2010 cheques generally cannot be corrected with a pen once a mistake is made. If the words and figures do not match, or if a date digit sits across two boxes, the bank may return the cheque. An online cheque printer does not replace judgement (you still check the payee and the amount) and it does not replace your signature. It removes avoidable slips.
