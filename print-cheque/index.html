Print cheque guide

How to print a cheque at home or in the office

A practical India guide to print a cheque on your own printer — for rent, suppliers or office payments — using genuine bank leaves and free Cheque Printer software.

Short answer: To print a cheque at home or in the office, use a genuine CTS-2010 leaf from your bank, a Windows PC and a printer. Download Cheque Printer, type the date, payee and amount, test on plain paper at A4 / 100% / no margins, then print on the leaf and sign by hand. Do not print cheques in a random website.

What you need before you print a cheque

Printing a cheque is ordinary office work, not a special banking service, but a few things have to be in place or the bank will not pay it.

  • A genuine cheque leaf from the account you will debit. Ask your branch or use your cheque book. Leaves are marked CTS-2010. Never use a photocopy, a scan printed on card, or a leaf from someone else’s book.
  • A printer — inkjet or laser — that can take a small sheet. A rear or manual-feed tray is ideal because you can see how the leaf goes in. Cassette trays built only for A4 can still work if you can place the leaf consistently against a guide.
  • A Windows 10 or 11 PC and the free Cheque Printer app, or a pen if you would rather write by hand. The app is useful when you print often, when you want the amount in words to be correct in lakh and crore, or when handwriting would look uneven.
  • A pen for the signature. Software must not and does not print the signature. Sign only after the printed ink is dry.

How to print a cheque at home

At home the usual jobs are rent, fees, a contractor, or a transfer that the payee still wants as a cheque. Work on a clean desk with enough light to compare a test print against a real leaf.

  1. Open Cheque Printer and choose your bank (SBI, HDFC, ICICI, Axis, Saraswat, or the standard CTS template) and the way your printer feeds: horizontal if the leaf goes in sideways, vertical if it goes in end-first.
  2. Type the date, payee and amount. Check the amount in words. If you just need the wording, the amount in words converter on this site will show it without opening the app.
  3. Choose A/C Payee unless you have a specific reason not to. An account-payee crossing tells the bank to credit only the named payee.
  4. Print a test on plain A4 using A4 paper size, 100% scale and no margins. Hold the print over a cheque leaf against a window or a lamp. If a field is slightly off, use the app’s calibration (described in the calibration guide) and test again. Do not skip this step the first time you use a new printer.
  5. Insert the real leaf the same way as the test sheet, print one copy, and wait for the ink to dry. Sign in the signature panel. Do not write or print on the MICR band along the bottom.

Keep cancelled test leaves and spoiled cheques; strike through them and write “CANCELLED” so they cannot be misused. Record the cheque number in your payment register the same way you would for a handwritten cheque.

How to print a cheque in the office

Shops, small companies, CA offices, societies, trusts, schools and clinics often print many cheques in a sitting: suppliers, salaries, refunds, statutory payments. The steps are the same as at home, with a little more process around them.

  • Keep the cheque book in the usual secure place. Only people who are allowed to issue cheques should operate the PC that has the app and the printer that is loaded with leaves.
  • Prepare a list of payees and amounts from your books first, then type them one by one. Check each preview against the invoice or voucher before you print.
  • Use the same printer and the same feed direction every day so you are not recalibrating between batches.
  • Several people can share the portable zip from a folder IT approves; settings stay on that PC.
  • After printing, the authorised signatory signs, and someone records the cheque numbers in the accounting system. Printing does not replace internal authorisation.

Rules that apply whether you print at home or at work

Indian bank cheques follow the CTS-2010 standard. A few rules catch people out:

  • No alterations. If you typo a payee or an amount, do not overwrite or use correction fluid. Cancel the leaf and print a new one. Banks are advised not to accept altered CTS cheques, other than a limited date validation.
  • Words and figures must match. The amount in words should use lakh and crore and end with “Only”. If they disagree, the cheque can be returned.
  • Validity is three months from the date on the cheque, including post-dated cheques once that date arrives.
  • The MICR band stays empty. That strip at the bottom is how clearing systems identify the leaf.
  • Only official leaves are valid. Printing a cheque-shaped picture on plain paper does not create a negotiable instrument.

Home printer tips: inkjet vs laser, horizontal vs vertical

Both inkjet and laser printers can print cheques. Laser (toner) is immediately smudge-proof. Pigment or “permanent” black ink on an inkjet is the better inkjet choice; cheap dye ink can run if the leaf gets wet. Do not stack printed cheques until they are dry.

Horizontal feed means the long edge goes in first (landscape). Vertical feed means an end goes in first, which is common on rear trays of many Canon, Epson and HP ink-tank printers. Pick the matching option in the app and stick to it. If the print comes out smaller than the leaf, the print dialog is almost certainly set to “Fit” instead of 100%.

Common mistakes when you print a cheque

  • Using “Fit to page” or letter paper instead of A4 at 100% with no margins.
  • Feeding the leaf slightly skewed, which shifts every field even when the software is correct.
  • Printing the first real cheque without a plain-paper test on a new printer.
  • Forgetting to sign, or signing before the ink dries and smearing the amount.
  • Writing extra numbers or a stamp in the MICR band.
  • Uploading a cancelled cheque or a payee list to an unofficial “print cheque online” website. Use the desktop online cheque printer instead.

If you would rather write the cheque by hand

That is still a good option for a one-off cheque. Use a black or blue pen, fill every box of the date, write the payee without a long gap, write the amount in words with lakh or crore, and draw a line through unused space. The converter can give you the wording even if you never install the app. When you print often, or when a cheque must look consistent for a business, cheque printing software saves time and reduces avoidable returns.

Frequently asked questions

Can I print a cheque at home in India?

Yes. You need a genuine CTS-2010 cheque leaf from your bank, a printer, and software or a steady hand to fill the date, payee and amount. Cheque Printer is free Windows software that places those fields for you. You must still sign the cheque by hand.

Can I print a cheque online without installing software?

This website does not offer in-browser printing, because cheque details should stay on your computer. Download the Cheque Printer desktop app and print from your PC. After install, the app works offline.

Is a printed cheque valid in India?

Yes. Banks accept cheques filled by a printer if the leaf is a genuine CTS-2010 leaf from your bank, the details are correct and complete, and it is signed by the account holder. Do not alter a printed cheque with a pen if you make a mistake; use a fresh leaf.

What printer do I need to print a cheque at home or in the office?

Any inkjet or laser printer that can feed a cheque leaf. A rear or manual tray is easier than a cassette designed only for A4. Use permanent black ink or toner. Print at A4, 100% scale, with no margins.

Can I photocopy a cheque and print on the copy?

No. You must use an official cheque leaf issued by your bank. Photocopies are not valid instruments, and CTS-2010 leaves have a VOID pantograph that appears on copies.

How long is a printed cheque valid?

In India a cheque is valid for three months from the date written on it. That is the same rule whether you write it by hand or print it.
