CTS-2010 cheque standard explained

Everything you need to know about India's CTS-2010 cheques, and how it affects printing them.

Download freeHow to print

Free · Windows 10/11 · Works offline

Short answer: CTS-2010 is RBI's standard for cheques cleared by image under the Cheque Truncation System. It fixes the paper, security features and layout: a leaf of about 202 × 92 mm, a "CTS-2010" mark, date boxes, a clear MICR band, and no alterations allowed.

What is CTS?

The Cheque Truncation System (CTS) clears cheques using images and data instead of physically sending the paper between banks. Because the clearing happens on images, cheques must be standardised so that every field is readable and tampering is visible.

Standard field positions

The order and approximate area of fields is the same on all CTS-2010 cheques: date top-right, "Pay" line below, two "Rupees" lines, the amount box at the right, signature area bottom-right, and the MICR band along the bottom. RBI's specification does not publish millimetre positions for every text field, and banks' designs vary by a few millimetres, which is why bank templates and calibration exist.

Rules for filling a CTS cheque

  • Write the date as DD MM YYYY, one digit per box.
  • Write the amount in words in lakh/crore, ending with "Only", with no gaps. Converter
  • Write the figures close to the ₹ symbol and end with "/-" or paise, e.g. 2,50,000.00.
  • Add the A/C Payee crossing to make it payable only into the payee's account.
  • Don't write on the MICR band, don't overwrite, and don't use correction fluid.
  • A cheque is valid for three months from its date.

This page summarises public RBI guidance in plain language. For the authoritative text, refer to the Reserve Bank of India's CTS-2010 standard and circulars.

Frequently asked questions

What is a CTS-2010 cheque?

A CTS-2010 cheque is a cheque that meets the Reserve Bank of India's 2010 standard for the Cheque Truncation System, in which banks clear cheques using images instead of moving the paper. It specifies paper, security features and a standard layout.

What is the size of a CTS-2010 cheque?

Standard CTS-2010 cheque leaves are about 202 mm × 92 mm.

Can I correct a mistake on a CTS cheque?

RBI has advised banks not to accept alterations on CTS-2010 cheques other than a date validation. If you make a mistake, cancel the leaf and use a fresh one. Printing avoids most mistakes.

How long is a cheque valid in India?

Cheques are valid for three months from the date written on them.

Can I print on the MICR band?

No. The MICR band at the bottom must be kept clear; it carries the cheque number, MICR code and transaction code read by the clearing system.
